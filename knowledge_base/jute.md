# Jute (Kharif, Monsoon Season)

Source: search aggregate of jute agronomy sources including Banglapedia
(https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Jute) and a BJRI Tossa Pat-8 fertilizer
study (https://www.sciencepg.com/article/10.11648/j.bio.20210905.13).
Retrieved: 2026-07-24

- Sowing window: Deshi jute is sown February-April in low-lying, moisture-
  retentive areas; Tossa jute is sown mid-March to April, with the wider
  producing-area window extending into May to catch pre-monsoon showers
  (optimal germination temperature 24-30°C).
- General fertilizer guidance: total N 40-60 kg/ha (split basal + top-dressing),
  P2O5 30-40 kg/ha, K2O 30-40 kg/ha.
- BJRI Tossa Pat-8 specific optimum: N:P:K:S = 100:10:60:20 kg/ha, reported as
  giving good growth and high fibre yield potential.
- Retting: after cutting, stalks are retted (soaked) for approximately 12-15
  days, after which fibre is separated by hand, washed, and sun-dried.
- Yield: average fibre yield around 1720 kg/ha in one study; peak fibre yield of
  up to 3242 kg/ha was recorded when harvested at the flowering stage (higher
  than early- or late-stage harvest).

## BAMIS Crop Weather Calendar (11 regions)

Source: BAMIS (Bangladesh Agro-Meteorological Information Portal) — Crop
Weather Calendar of Jute, covering Rangpur, Rajshahi, Mymensingh, Khulna,
Jashore, Faridpur, Dinajpur, Dhaka, Cumilla, Bogura, and Barishal regions.
(Unlike the rice calendars in this knowledge base, BAMIS does not publish a
Jute calendar for Sylhet, Rangamati, or Chattogram — plausibly because those
hill/high-rainfall regions are less suited to jute cultivation.)
Retrieved: 2026-07-24

- BAMIS-wide crop duration: 110-120 days, uniform across all 11 regions —
  this is a separate figure from the BJRI Tossa Pat-8 variety data above and
  should be read as the general BAMIS calendar duration, not variety-specific.
- Regional sowing windows (std. week range): most regions start in
  March (std. week ~9-11): Rangpur, Rajshahi, Faridpur, Dinajpur, Cumilla,
  Bogura, Dhaka, Barishal. Mymensingh, Khulna, and Jashore start later, in
  mid-April (std. week ~14) — consistent with the wider Tossa jute sowing
  window (mid-March to May) noted above extending into these regions.
  Harvest generally falls June-July depending on region.

### Favorable weather conditions (mostly consistent, with some regional variation)

- Temperature: 15-25°C in the early/vegetative window, 18-35°C through the
  rest of the season — same in every region.
- Photoperiod: varies by region, roughly 3.0-8.5 hours depending on stage —
  e.g. Rangpur 4.0-8.0 hr, Rajshahi/Dinajpur/Khulna/Bogura 4.0-8.5 hr,
  Mymensingh 3.5-7.5 hr, Barishal 3.0-7.5 hr. Treat the exact range as
  region-specific rather than a single national figure.
- Relative humidity: 60-90% in most regions, but 65-90% (Mymensingh) or
  70-90% (Khulna, Cumilla, Barishal) in a few — the wetter regions have a
  higher RH floor.
- Rainfall: around 1000-1500 mm favorable for the season, nationwide.
- Phase-wise water requirement across 3 broad growth phases: 210 mm, 310 mm,
  95 mm — total ≈ 615 mm/season, uniform across all 11 regions. This is a
  simpler 3-phase breakdown than the 5-phase profile used for the rice crops
  in this knowledge base (see aman_rice.md, aus_rice.md, boro_rice.md).

### Congenial weather conditions for pests & diseases

- Universal (listed in all 11 regions, naming varies slightly by region —
  e.g. "Jute Semi-lopper"/"Jute Semi-loper", "Jute Hairy Caterpillar"/"Hairy
  Caterpiller"):
  - Stem rot: temperature 15-40°C, warm and dry weather.
  - Blank band: temperature 25-28°C.
  - Leaf mosaic (virus): temperature 20-30°C and RH around 80% (favors the
    whitefly vector).
  - Seedling blight: higher soil temperatures.
  - Field Cricket: loamy/sandy-loam soil, dry weather, temperature 27-32°C.
  - Jute Hairy Caterpillar: high temperature, high humidity, cloudy weather.
  - Jute Semi-lopper: RH 65-85%, temperature 28-34°C, rainfall 20-100 mm.
  - Jute Yellow mite: dry and hot weather.
- Die back: temperature 15-40°C, warm and dry weather (identical trigger to
  Stem rot). Listed for 4 of 11 regions — Rangpur, Mymensingh, Jashore,
  Dinajpur.
- Root knot/rot (naming varies — "Root knot," "Root rot," and once "Root
  kont," likely an OCR typo for "knot"): high temperature and prolonged
  water logging. Listed for 6 of 11 regions — Mymensingh, Khulna, Jashore,
  Faridpur, Dinajpur, Barishal; absent from Rangpur, Rajshahi, Dhaka,
  Cumilla, and Bogura.

### Weather warning thresholds (BAMIS, same nationwide — notably different structure from the rice crops)

- Rainfall: seasonal total <1000 mm triggers a warning (a drought-style
  threshold, unlike the rice calendars' per-day rainfall triggers).
- Relative humidity: <60% triggers a warning.
- Water logging: flagged as a risk in the mid-season window.
- Wet spell: >15 mm sustained for 19 days is flagged (early and mid-season).
- Drought: 15-20 days without rain.
- Hail storm: flagged as a risk at every stage.
- Cloudy weather: flagged as a risk in mid- and late-season windows.
- High wind: >50 km/hr (early season), >40 km/hr (mid/late season).
- Temperature: minimum <10°C and maximum >32°C (early season); minimum
  <15°C and maximum >43°C (mid/late season) — a much higher heat-warning
  ceiling than any rice crop in this knowledge base, reflecting jute's
  greater heat tolerance.

## Notes for the agent

- Jute is a monsoon (Kharif) crop with comparatively low fertilizer need but a
  distinct post-harvest step (retting) that needs standing water access, not
  just field water — relevant when checking water availability against this
  crop.
- Harvest timing (flowering stage) materially affects fibre yield per the
  source data — worth reflecting in season-plan harvest-window guidance.
- Unit note: figures are kg/hectare. 1 hectare = 2.471 acres.
