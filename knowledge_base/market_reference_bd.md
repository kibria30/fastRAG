# Bangladesh Fertilizer Prices and Crop Yields/Market Prices (Reference)

These figures ground the financial projection math (cost, revenue, profit, ROI).
Where a source did not give a clean number, or where sources disagreed, this is
stated explicitly rather than inventing one — see "Data quality" notes per crop.
Prices in Bangladesh's foodgrain system exist at several distinct levels
(government procurement price, open-market wholesale, open-market retail,
subsidized OMS sale price) which are **not interchangeable** — see the
"Understanding the price levels" note at the end of this file.

## Fertilizer retail prices (subsidized government rate)

Source: New Age BD, "Bangladesh government raises fertiliser prices by Tk 5 per
kg" (10 Apr 2023) — https://www.newagebd.net/article/199179/bangladesh-government-raises-fertiliser-prices-by-tk-5-per-kg ;
corroborated as still the prevailing administered rate a year later by
Bonik Barta, "Fertilizer import cost drops 51% but no price cut yet for
farmers" (4 Apr 2024) — https://www.bonikbarta.com/home/news_description/379313/Fertilizer-import-cost-drops-51-but-no-price-cut-yet-for-farmers- ;
and further corroborated (with a note that farmers are often overcharged above
the administered rate) by The Daily Star, "Farmers forced to pay more than
subsidised rates for fertiliser" — https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/farmers-forced-pay-more-subsidised-rates-fertiliser-3810946
Retrieved: 2026-07-25

- Urea: 27 BDT/kg (retail, subsidized, farmer level)
- TSP (Triple Super Phosphate): 27 BDT/kg
- DAP (Diammonium Phosphate): 21 BDT/kg
- MoP (Muriate of Potash): 20 BDT/kg
- Data quality note: this Tk 5/kg hike took effect 10 April 2023 and was still
  the quoted administered rate as of the April 2024 and later sources above —
  treat it as stable/current absent a newer hike announcement, not as a 2026-
  specific figure.
- Data quality note: as of the 2024/2025 sources, dealers in some areas were
  charging farmers Tk 3-4/kg above the administered rate, citing supply
  shortages — the administered rate above is the official price, not
  necessarily what every farmer pays at the field level.
- For context, the same April 2024 source cites the *international market*
  price (i.e. before subsidy) at roughly Urea 48, DAP 70, TSP 50, MoP 60
  BDT/kg — the gap is the size of the government subsidy per kg.
- Gypsum, ZnSO4, and boric acid retail prices were not found in these sources —
  treat their cost contribution as a smaller, unsourced estimate in the finance
  module, not a sourced figure.

## Government procurement prices (paddy/rice/wheat)

Source: Food Planning and Monitoring Unit (FPMU), Ministry of Food —
Fortnightly Foodgrain Outlook, Issue No. 465 (25 June 2026) —
http://www.mofood.gov.bd ; corroborated by The Daily Star, "Govt sticks to old
Boro price despite high production cost" —
https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/govt-sticks-old-boro-price-despite-high-production-cost-4158386 ,
Jagonews24, "Govt to procure Boro paddy at Tk 36, rice at Tk 49 per kg" —
https://www.jagonews24.com/en/national/news/92072 , and (for Aman) New Age BD,
"Govt to buy aman rice at Tk 46, paddy at Tk 33 per kg" (prior-season figure,
for trend) — https://www.newagebd.net/post/commodities/249643/govt-to-buy-aman-rice-at-tk-46-paddy-at-tk-33-per-kg
Retrieved: 2026-07-25

- **Boro season 2026** (procurement window 3 May – 31 Aug 2026): paddy 36
  BDT/kg, parboiled rice 49 BDT/kg, atap (sun-dried) rice 48 BDT/kg. Government
  target ~1,625 thousand MT rice-equivalent; 770.2 thousand MT procured as of
  18 June 2026. This price was held flat versus the prior Boro season despite
  farmer complaints of rising cultivation costs (Daily Star).
- **Aman season 2025-26** (procurement window 20 Nov 2025 – 28 Feb 2026): paddy
  34 BDT/kg, parboiled rice 50 BDT/kg, (regular) boiled rice 49 BDT/kg — up
  Tk 1/kg (paddy) and Tk 3/kg (rice) versus the prior Aman season.
- Data quality note: the previous version of this file used a single 26 BDT/kg
  paddy price for both Boro and Aman. That figure is now superseded — Boro and
  Aman have distinct, and different, government procurement prices (36 vs 34
  BDT/kg for paddy in the 2025-26/2026 seasons), and both are meaningfully
  above the older 26 BDT/kg reference point.
- These are **government procurement (floor/support) prices**, i.e. what the
  state pays farmers who sell into the public stock — not the open-market
  price a farmer gets selling to a private trader (see next section).

## Open-market retail/wholesale prices (Dhaka city, DAM data via FPMU)

Source: FPMU Fortnightly Foodgrain Outlook, Issue No. 465 (25 June 2026),
citing Department of Agricultural Marketing (DAM) daily price data.
Reporting fortnight ending 18 June 2026. Retrieved: 2026-07-25

- **Coarse rice** (Aman-Ufsi-Coarse variety, Dhaka city): retail 51.0 BDT/kg,
  wholesale 45.0 BDT/kg (retail was 4.7% lower and wholesale 9.1% lower than
  the same period a year earlier).
- **Atta (wheat flour)**: retail 43.5 BDT/kg, wholesale 36.5 BDT/kg. Note this
  is milled flour, not raw wheat grain — do not use it interchangeably with a
  farmgate wheat grain price (see Wheat below).
- **Subsidized Open Market Sale (OMS)**, a separate government safety-net
  channel (not the same as procurement or open-market price): rice sold at a
  flat 30 BDT/kg and atta at 24 BDT/kg, capped at 5 kg per beneficiary per
  grain.

## Crop yields and market (farmgate/wholesale) prices, by crop

Source: Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) Yearbook of Agricultural
Statistics (bbs.gov.bd) yield reporting, BRRI variety trial data, and
Tridge/Selina Wamucii market price aggregators, via search aggregate.
Retrieved: 2026-07-24/25

- **Boro rice (paddy)**: average yield ~6.0 t/ha (BRRI varieties, 2021-22
  season average). Price: use the government Boro procurement price above
  (36 BDT/kg, 2026 season) as the sourced reference; a farmer selling on the
  open market rather than into government procurement may realize a somewhat
  different price, which this knowledge base does not have a sourced figure
  for.
- **Aman rice (paddy)**: average yield 2.57 t/ha (BBS, FY2020-21 national
  average) — notably lower than Boro since it is rain-fed rather than
  irrigated. Price: use the government Aman procurement price above (34
  BDT/kg, 2025-26 season) as the sourced reference, distinct from Boro's price
  (correcting the earlier version of this file, which used one shared paddy
  price for both seasons).
- **Wheat**: yield reference 2.78 t/ha (BBS 2012 national average / BARI
  variety range 2-3 t/ha). Data quality note: a more recent (2024) national
  average figure of 0.376 t/ha also appeared in search results, but this looks
  like it reflects a collapse in wheat-sown area/national output mix rather
  than typical per-field productivity, so it was not used. Price: two
  candidate figures exist and are **not the same thing** — raw wheat grain
  farmgate price ~32.45 BDT/kg (Selina Wamucii, dated May 2026) versus milled
  atta (flour) retail/wholesale price of 43.5/36.5 BDT/kg (DAM/FPMU, June
  2026, see above). Use the farmgate grain figure (32.45 BDT/kg) for a
  farmer's crop-sale revenue calculation; the atta price reflects a
  downstream, processed product.
- **Potato**: yield 38-54 t/ha (TCRC/BARI trial range, see potato.md). Price:
  ~30 BDT/kg (farmgate ~27.6 BDT/kg per Selina Wamucii, wholesale range 25-45
  BDT/kg — midpoint used). Data quality note: the Selina Wamucii farmgate
  figure's underlying date stamp is inconsistent between page metadata (Jan
  2021) and its "latest check" date (Jun 2026) — treat this price as an
  approximate reference, not a precise point-in-time figure.
- **Maize**: yield 7.73 t/ha (BARI hybrid maize trial, see maize.md). Price:
  data quality note — search results for maize retail price were inconsistent
  (ranging ~52-125 BDT/kg retail in Dhaka/Chittagong per one aggregate source,
  and a separate wholesale estimate of roughly USD 0.34-0.81/kg, i.e. ~37-89
  BDT/kg) — both ranges are unusually wide and not fully reconciled with each
  other. Treated as unreliable; the finance module uses a conservative
  approximate price (~30 BDT/kg) flagged as an assumption, not a sourced
  figure.
- **Jute (fibre)**: yield ~1.72 t/ha average, up to 3.24 t/ha at peak
  (flowering-stage harvest, see jute.md). Price: The Financial Express,
  "Jute prices hit record high" (1 Sep 2025) —
  https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/last-page/jute-prices-hit-record-high-1756663808 —
  reports raw jute selling at an all-time-high 4,000-4,300 BDT/maund (37.32
  kg) across major producing areas (Pabna, Tangail, Rajbari, Faridpur,
  Gopalganj, Shariatpur, Madaripur); converted at 1 maund = 37.32 kg, this is
  ~107-115 BDT/kg. This supersedes the earlier version of this file's "up to
  4,500 BDT/maund" figure, which lacked a specific source/date — the new
  figure is lower but has a named source, date, and named regions.

## Understanding the price levels (important for the agent)

Bangladesh's foodgrain market has at least four distinct price levels that
must not be conflated in financial projections:

1. **Government procurement price** — a floor price the state pays farmers who
   sell into public stock (e.g. Boro paddy 36 BDT/kg, 2026). Only a portion of
   total production moves through this channel.
2. **Open-market wholesale price** — what traders pay/charge each other (e.g.
   coarse rice wholesale 45 BDT/kg, Dhaka, June 2026).
3. **Open-market retail price** — what a consumer pays in a city market (e.g.
   coarse rice retail 51 BDT/kg, Dhaka, June 2026). This is a consumer cost
   figure, not a farmer revenue figure.
4. **Subsidized OMS price** — a safety-net sale price to consumers (e.g. rice
   30 BDT/kg), unrelated to farmer revenue.

For a farmer's financial projection (expected revenue at harvest), the
relevant figure is generally the **government procurement price** (if the crop
and season have one) or a **farmgate/wholesale** reference — never the
consumer retail price, which is inflated by margins the farmer does not
receive.

## Notes for the agent

- Prices in this file are national reference points from the sources and
  dates cited per section, not live, location-specific quotes — real-time
  market price lookup (sell-now/store/wait intelligence) is a Tier 2 feature,
  not built at Tier 0.
- Where this file says a price/yield is an "assumption," "approximate," or
  "data quality" caveat rather than a clean sourced figure, the finance module
  must be able to say so in its output (data provenance should be visible, not
  silently blended with sourced figures).
- Government procurement prices change by season and by year (see the Boro
  vs. Aman, and year-over-year, differences documented above) — if this file
  is not refreshed, treat procurement prices as stale after roughly one
  procurement cycle (a few months) and prefer re-checking FPMU's Fortnightly
  Foodgrain Outlook (published biweekly at mofood.gov.bd) over trusting an old
  number indefinitely.
