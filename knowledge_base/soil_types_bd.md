- Bangladesh is divided into 30 agro-ecological zones (AEZs), further split
  into 88 agro-ecological sub-regions and 535 agro-ecological units (SRDI
  framework). AEZs are defined by four elements: physiography, soils, land
  level in relation to flooding (highland, medium highland, medium lowland,
  lowland, very lowland, bottomland), and agro-climatology.
- Data quality correction: an earlier version of this file said "seven
  general soil types occur nationally." That figure could not be
  corroborated and is superseded — the SRDI/FAO-UNDP classification actually
  identifies **21 general soil types** across three physiographic regions
  (see below). Treat any prior "seven soil types" claim as wrong.

## The three physiographic regions and 21 general soil types

- **Floodplain soils** — 78.96% of land area (~9.72 million ha), by far the
  dominant physiography. 14 general types: calcareous alluvium,
  non-calcareous alluvium, calcareous brown floodplain soils, calcareous
  grey floodplain soils, calcareous dark grey floodplain soils,
  non-calcareous grey floodplain soils, non-calcareous brown floodplain
  soils, non-calcareous dark grey floodplain soils, black terai soils, acid
  basin clays, acid sulphate soils, peat, grey piedmont soils, and made-land.
  Non-calcareous grey floodplain soils alone occupy roughly a quarter of the
  country's total area and are among the most agriculturally productive
  soils nationally.
- **Hill soils** — 12.69% of land area (~1.56 million ha), essentially one
  general type (brown hill soils), found in the Chittagong Hill Tracts and
  Sylhet's northern/eastern hills. Hill soils are frequently very strongly
  to extremely acidic (older soils where topsoil acidification has
  progressed furthest).
- **Terrace soils** — 8.35% of land area (~1.03 million ha), on the
  Pleistocene terraces (Barind Tract, Madhupur Tract). 6 general types:
  shallow red-brown terrace soils, deep red-brown terrace soils, brown
  mottled terrace soils, shallow grey terrace soils, deep grey terrace
  soils, and grey valley soils. Like hill soils, terrace soils trend acidic.
- Acid sulphate soils (a floodplain type, found mainly in tidal/estuarine
  areas) are a specific hazard: they become extremely acidic if exposed to
  air (e.g. through drainage or excavation) — worth flagging if a farmer's
  land is in a tidal floodplain zone and reports drainage/excavation work.

## Regional texture examples (illustrative, not exhaustive)

- Clay soils: parts of Faridpur, Rajbari, Kushtia, Madaripur, Shariatpur.
- Loam soils: parts of Mymensingh, Sherpur, Tangail.
- Sandy loam: parts of Dhaka, Manikganj, Gazipur, Narayanganj, Narsingdi,
  Rangpur.
- Sandy soils: parts of Cox's Bazar.

## Coastal soil salinity

Source: SRDI, Soil Salinity Report (November 2010).

- Of about 1.689 million ha of coastal land, about 1.056 million ha (~62.5%)
  are affected by soil salinity of varying degree, across 49 upazilas in 19
  districts.
- Severity breakdown (SRDI classes S1-S5, million ha): very slight (S1)
  0.328, slight (S2) 0.274, moderate (S3) 0.189, strong (S4) 0.161, very
  strong (S5) 0.101.
- Trend: salinity-affected area grew from 0.833 to 1.056 million ha between
  1973 and 2009 — a 26% increase, i.e. about 0.223 million ha of previously
  unaffected land became saline over that period.
- Regional concentration: 22-47% of land affected by salinity in Satkhira,
  Khulna, and Patuakhali; Barguna is worst-affected at 48-76%.
- This directly affects crop choice: salinity-tolerant varieties/crops
  matter far more for farmers in these southern/southwestern coastal
  districts than elsewhere in the country, and worsening trend means an
  older regional assumption may understate current risk.

## SRDI Land Suitability Matrix

SRDI's Land Suitability Matrix scores land/soil quality using: land type,
relief, water recession pattern, drainage, soil texture, topsoil
consistency, available soil moisture, soil pH, and salinity.

## Notes for the agent

- This is a coarse, illustrative regional mapping, not a field-level soil
  test — when a farmer reports their own soil type directly (as Tier 0
  intake asks), trust their stated value over any regional inference from
  this table.
- Useful mainly as a plausibility check, or as a fallback when a farmer knows
  their district but is unsure of their exact soil type or its salinity/pH
  characteristics.
- If a farmer is in a coastal/tidal district (Satkhira, Khulna, Patuakhali,
  Barguna, Bagerhat, Barishal, Noakhali, Cox's Bazar, etc.), proactively
  consider salinity as a limiting factor for crop choice — this is a much
  more decision-relevant regional signal than soil texture alone.
- Don't confuse the 21 *general soil types* (physical/chemical
  classification) with the 30 *agro-ecological zones* (a broader planning
  unit combining physiography, soil, flooding, and climate) — they're
  different, overlapping classification systems from the same SRDI
  framework, not a 1:1 mapping.
